how to read this

Rows are lines of the tune, stacked so their beats line up: read down a column to compare line 1 with line 2. Each row: the beat ruler (click a bar number to loop that bar, shift-click to loop a run of bars), the chords, the melody (pulse1), your letter notation (amber cells are where this line's rhythm differs from the line it echoes), pulse2, the triangle bass, the noise drums, and a tension strip.

Beat shading: alternating bands are beat groups (a 7/8 bar is 2+2+3 eighths here, worked out from where the kick and snare land). The strongest line is the downbeat.

Tension (0 calm to 1 tense) for a note = 0.4 × how far its chord is from home (Lerdahl's chord distance) + 0.4 × how far the note is from its chord (root, fifth, chord tone, scale tone, outside the key) + 0.2 × how hard it rubs against whatever else is sounding. The strip under each row draws it: the colored line is the melody, the blue fill is the chord, dots are surprise (a note the song hasn't taught you to expect yet sits high).

Lines between melody notes: thickness is how far the jump is in pitch; color is how far it is around the circle of fifths (cool = near, like C to G; hot = far, like C to F#). So C, E and G look alike, and C next to C# looks far.

Color modes: tension colors notes by the score above; chord role greens chord tones, yellows scale tones outside the chord, reds notes outside the key; circle of fifths gives each pitch a fixed hue around the circle (Scriabin did this), so neighbors on the circle look alike.
